# Static Longitudinal Stability — Cone-Body Quadcopter

## 1. Configuration and Free-Body Diagram

The vehicle is modeled as a body of revolution (an axisymmetric shell/cone, generated by revolving a symmetric NACA profile) with pylon-mounted motors on either side. All station distances are measured aft from the nose along the body axis:

- `l_s` — station of the shell/cone's aerodynamic center (AC)
- `l_cg` — station of the center of gravity
- `l_p` — station of the pylons' aerodynamic center
- `l_t` — vertical separation of the top/bottom motor pairs from the centerline

At the shell AC: lift `L_s`, drag `D_s`, and moment about the AC `M_ac,s`.
At the CG: weight `W`, acting down.
At the pylon AC: lift `L_p`, drag `D_p`, and moment about the AC `M_ac,p`.
At the pylon station, four motors are mounted, equally spaced vertically (`±l_t`) from the centerline. Each pair produces thrust `T`; the bottom pair carries an additional differential term `δT`, introduced to balance the nose-heavy moment created by the forward CG.

The whole vehicle is pitched at angle `α` relative to the flight path. All quantities in this derivation (`L_s`, `D_s`, `M_ac,s`, `L_p`, `D_p`, `M_ac,p`) are treated as general functions of `α` — no assumption is made about `α` being small or near zero anywhere below.

## 2. Assumptions

1. **Steady, unaccelerated, straight flight** — all EOM below are for trim (ΣF = 0, ΣM = 0), not a maneuvering or dynamic case.
2. **All body-generated lift comes from the shell** — in the "ideal" case, motor thrust is assumed to go entirely into propulsion, with none diverted to supporting weight; all lift is aerodynamic (shell + pylons).
3. **`δT` is a control input, not a function of `α`** — it exists to trim the pitching moment from the forward CG offset, and for the stability derivative below it is held fixed while `α` is perturbed (stick-fixed condition). It is not re-solved as part of a moment balance at each `α`.
4. **No small-angle simplification.** Unlike a first-pass derivation, `cos α` and `sin α` are carried through exactly. This matters here because the vehicle may operate at trim angles far from zero (the forward CG and cone-body lift generation make this plausible), so a small-angle result would only be valid in a narrow neighborhood of whatever α it was linearized about. Carrying the full trig terms means the resulting neutral point is valid across the whole α range the aerodynamic data covers.
5. **Drag moment-arm contributions are second-order relative to lift.** `D_s`, `D_p` terms are dropped from the final stability derivative, consistent with standard practice in conventional aircraft stability derivations. This is a simplification, not a general truth, and is worth revisiting for this geometry (see Section 6) — it is independent of the small-angle question and is kept even in the non-linearized version below.
6. **No physical mean chord `c̄` exists for the shell.** Being a body of revolution, the shell cannot be nondimensionalized the conventional way (`q·S·c̄`). The derivation below avoids needing one by working in dimensional force/moment terms and solving directly for a neutral point location.

## 3. Equations of Motion (Trim)

**Sum of forces, X (drag axis):**
```
D_s + D_p − (2T + δT) cos α = 0
```

**Sum of forces, Y (lift axis):**
```
L_s + L_p + (2T + δT) cos α − W = 0
```

**Sum of moments about the CG:**
```
M(x=l_cg, α) = L_s(l_cg − l_s) cos α + D_s(l_cg − l_s) sin α
             − L_p(l_p − l_cg) cos α − D_p(l_p − l_cg) sin α
             + δT · l_t + M_ac,s + M_ac,p = 0
```

## 4. Why the Conventional Nondimensionalization Fails Here

For a conventional aircraft, the moment equation is nondimensionalized by `½ρV²S·c̄`, where `c̄` is the mean aerodynamic chord of the wing. This works because a wing is (locally) a lifting surface with a well-defined chord and a `Cm_ac` defined per unit chord.

The shell here is a body of revolution generating lift through slender-body/cross-flow effects, not circulation over a chord — there is no leading/trailing edge to define a chord from. Forcing a chord-based reference length onto this geometry produces a number with no physical meaning.

**Resolution used below:** stay in dimensional form (N, N·m) and solve directly for the neutral point location, which requires no reference length at all.

## 5. Neutral-Point Derivation (Exact, No Small-Angle Assumption)

### 5.1 Setup

The neutral point `x_np(α)` is the CG location at which `dM_cg/dα = 0` at a given trim angle `α` — the stability boundary at that flight condition. Because nothing here has been linearized in α, `x_np` is in general itself a function of α, not a single fixed number for the vehicle. To find it, replace the fixed `l_cg` in the moment equation with a free variable `x`, differentiate the whole expression with respect to `α` while keeping every `cos α` / `sin α` term intact, and solve for the `x` that zeroes the derivative at that particular α.

```
M(x, α) = L_s(α)(x − l_s) cos α + D_s(α)(x − l_s) sin α
        − L_p(α)(l_p − x) cos α − D_p(α)(l_p − x) sin α
        + δT · l_t + M_ac,s(α) + M_ac,p(α)
```

### 5.2 Differentiate with respect to α

`δT · l_t` is dropped immediately: `δT` is held fixed while perturbing `α` (Assumption 3), so its derivative is zero.

Every remaining term is a product of an aerodynamic quantity (a function of `α`) and a trig function (also a function of `α`), so the product rule applies to each:

```
dM/dα = (dL_s/dα)(x−l_s) cos α − L_s(x−l_s) sin α
      + (dD_s/dα)(x−l_s) sin α + D_s(x−l_s) cos α
      − (dL_p/dα)(l_p−x) cos α + L_p(l_p−x) sin α
      − (dD_p/dα)(l_p−x) sin α − D_p(l_p−x) cos α
      + dM_ac,s/dα + dM_ac,p/dα
```

No terms have been dropped yet — every `sin α` and `cos α` factor from the original moment equation and from differentiating the trig functions themselves is still present. This expression is valid at any α within the range the aerodynamic data (`L_s`, `D_s`, `M_ac,s`, `L_p`, `D_p`, `M_ac,p`, and their α-derivatives) actually covers.

### 5.3 Drop drag terms (Assumption 5)

The only simplification applied from here on is dropping the drag-arm terms `D_s(x−l_s) cos α` and `D_p(l_p−x) cos α`, which are second-order relative to the corresponding lift-slope terms. Everything else — all `sin α` terms arising from `L_s`, `L_p`, and their derivatives — is retained, since these do not vanish except at the specific angles where `sin α = 0`, and no such restriction is being assumed:

```
dM/dα ≈ (dL_s/dα)(x−l_s) cos α − L_s(x−l_s) sin α
       − (dL_p/dα)(l_p−x) cos α + L_p(l_p−x) sin α
       + dM_ac,s/dα + dM_ac,p/dα
```

### 5.4 Set to zero and solve for x = x_np(α)

Group all terms multiplying `x` together, and all terms independent of `x` together. Every term above is linear in `x` (each is either `x` times something, or `l_s`/`l_p` times something), so this is a straightforward rearrangement, not an approximation:

**Terms multiplying x:**
```
x · [ (dL_s/dα) cos α − L_s sin α + (dL_p/dα) cos α − L_p sin α ]
```

**Terms independent of x:**
```
− l_s·(dL_s/dα) cos α + l_s·L_s sin α − l_p·(dL_p/dα) cos α + l_p·L_p sin α + dM_ac,s/dα + dM_ac,p/dα
```

Setting `dM/dα = 0` and solving for `x`:

```
x_np(α) =
    [ l_s·(dL_s/dα) cos α − l_s·L_s sin α + l_p·(dL_p/dα) cos α − l_p·L_p sin α − dM_ac,s/dα − dM_ac,p/dα ]
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    [ (dL_s/dα) cos α − L_s sin α + (dL_p/dα) cos α − L_p sin α ]
```

This is the general, non-linearized neutral point. It reduces exactly to the earlier small-angle result if `cos α → 1` and `sin α → 0` are substituted in — that version is just this one evaluated at the special case α ≈ 0. Away from α ≈ 0, the `L_s sin α` and `L_p sin α` terms in both numerator and denominator are no longer negligible and must be kept, which is why they were retained through every step above.

### 5.5 Static margin

```
Static margin(α) = x_np(α) − l_cg
```

Positive → statically stable at that trim α, using the same sign convention as a conventional aircraft. Because `x_np` now depends on α, the vehicle's stability margin can in principle change across its operating envelope — it is not guaranteed to be a single fixed number, which is a direct consequence of not assuming small α, and is worth checking against the Roskam data across the full trim range once available.

## 6. Open Items / Things to Revisit

- **Behavior where the denominator crosses zero.** The denominator of `x_np(α)`, `(dL_s/dα) cos α − L_s sin α + (dL_p/dα) cos α − L_p sin α`, is itself a function of α and is not guaranteed to stay away from zero across the full α range — unlike the small-angle version, this is a genuine possibility once `sin α` terms are non-negligible. Any α where this expression passes through zero is a point where the neutral point location is undefined (goes to infinity) rather than merely large; this should be checked against the actual Roskam-derived curves once available, not assumed away.
- **Drag terms dropped without geometry-specific justification.** Assumption 5 is imported from conventional aircraft practice, independent of the α range being considered. Given the unconventional shell shape, it is worth checking whether `D_s`, `D_p` are actually negligible next to the lift-slope terms once real Roskam-derived numbers are available.
- **Pylon aero may depend on prop wash, not just freestream α.** If `L_p`, `D_p`, `M_ac,p` are functions of rotor RPM/thrust in addition to α (likely, if pylons sit in the slipstream), and if T or δT varies with trim α, there is a cross term `(dL_p/dT)(dT/dα)` that this derivation does not capture. Confirm whether the pylon aero data being generated already assumes fixed RPM.
- **Roskam Class I/II data comes nondimensionalized against a wing that doesn't exist here.** Multhopp-method fuselage contributions (used for `dL_s/dα`, `dM_ac,s/dα`) are normally reported as `CL_α` / `Cm_α`, nondimensionalized by the *wing's* S and c̄ — which this vehicle doesn't have. The dimensional slopes (`∂L/∂α`, `∂M/∂α`) are needed directly, or the exact `S_ref`/`l_ref` Roskam used, so the coefficients can be converted back to dimensional form before use in Section 5.
- **`l_s`, `l_p` conventions may not match Roskam's.** Roskam's fuselage-AC convention may not coincide with the geometric station assumed here — confirm before combining values from different sources.
- **Validity range of the underlying aerodynamic data.** This derivation is only as good as the α range over which `L_s(α)`, `L_p(α)`, `M_ac,s(α)`, `M_ac,p(α)` and their derivatives are actually valid (e.g. Roskam Class I/II methods typically break down near or past stall). The formula itself places no bound on α, so the aerodynamic data's own domain of validity is the real constraint on where `x_np(α)` can be trusted.
- **Nondimensional coefficient form, if needed later** (e.g. for integration into a 6-DOF sim): since there is no chord, a missile/body-of-revolution convention could be used instead (per Nielsen, *Missile Aerodynamics*, or DATCOM) — e.g. `S_ref` = max frontal cross-sectional area, `l_ref` = overall body length or max diameter. The resulting `Cm_α` would not be physically comparable to a conventional aircraft's and would only be useful internally, consistently applied across all component terms. Alternatively, Munk's slender-body result nondimensionalizes the shell's moment contribution by dynamic pressure × body volume (`q·∀`) rather than area × length, which ties more directly to the physics generating `L_s`/`M_ac,s` — though it only covers the shell term, not the pylons.
